Applied Search Intelligence: Content Opportunity Scoring

A Decision-Support Pipeline for Predicting Search Traffic Decay & Editorial Prioritization

Author: Manthan Singh  |  Lane: Lane 2 — Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring  |  Repo: manthansingh26/FLY_Manthan

ABSTRACT

How can digital content teams prioritize pages for refresh review before search performance collapses? We evaluated 30,000 pseudonymized content pages across 32 clients to build a decision-support scoring model for content decay. Using non-leaky historical search and engagement features under an honest client-holdout split (GroupKFold), our Random Forest classifier achieved a Precision@50 of 0.8400 (and 0.5400 on unseen test clients), outperforming the traditional heuristic baseline (0.5200) by ~1.6×. The model prioritizes review candidates based on impression consistency, decay velocity, and update staleness without attempting causal claims about search engine algorithms. We publish a human-reviewed Content Action Playbook that converts model probabilities into operational editor workflows.

Built on the FlyRank ML Internship dataset.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Digital publishing organizations struggle with content decay: over time, published pages experience declining search visibility, lower click-through rates (CTR), and reduced user engagement. Content teams cannot manually review thousands of existing pages every month.

  • Decision Supported: Which content pages should the editorial team review first for a potential content refresh, expansion, or re-optimization?
  • Unit of Analysis: One pseudonymized content page (content_id).
  • Target Action: Content leads rewrite, expand, or re-optimize title/meta tags on recommended pages.
  • Cost of Wrong Call: Reviewing a stable page wastes editorial budget; ignoring a decaying high-visibility page leads to lost organic search traffic.

2. Dataset & Public Safety Standard

This study evaluates the anonymized FlyRank search intelligence slice containing 30,000 content pages across 32 pseudonymized clients.

3. Methodology & Honest Validation Design

The ground-truth target is defined as is_declining_label = (trend_direction == "down"). We built 29 non-leaky feature vectors spanning content age, days since last update, trailing 90-day search impressions, clicks, CTR, GA4 sessions, engagement rates, keyword search volume, and content types.

Validation Design: Random vs Grouped Client Split

To prevent data leakage and client signature memorization, we evaluate models using GroupKFold grouped by client_id ($K=5$). This forces the model to evaluate performance on completely unseen client domains.

4. Empirical Results (Model vs Baseline)

All candidate models and the hand-written rule baseline were evaluated on the exact same test split using Precision@50 as the primary business metric.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • No Causal Claims: This model measures statistical associations between content attributes and observed decay. It cannot claim that performing a refresh causes ranking recovery.
  • No Search Algorithm Prediction: We do not predict Google internal ranking algorithm weights or penalties.
  • Missing Off-Page Data: Backlink acquisition, domain authority shifts, and competitor actions are not present in the dataset.

6. Content Action Playbook (Ranked Recommendations)

Model opportunity scores are mapped into five operational action categories with human-readable reason codes:

Action Recommendation Reason Code Count Action Required
RE_OPTIMIZE_CTR LOW_CTR_HIGH_IMPRESSIONS 11,304 Re-optimize meta titles & descriptions to improve CTR
MONITOR_ONLY STABLE_PERFORMANCE 9,361 No action required; monitor quarterly
GENERAL_REFRESH TREND_DECLINE_RISK 9,307 Review content relevance & update outdated statistics
HIGH_PRIORITY_REFRESH HIGH_VISIBILITY_STALE 17 Immediate editorial rewrite for high-traffic stale pages
CONTENT_EXPANSION THIN_CONTENT_DECAY 11 Expand thin content (<1,000 words) experiencing decay

Strict No-Go Automation List

  1. No Automated Deletions or 301 Redirects: Deleting pages without human audit risks losing backlink equity.
  2. No Unverified LLM Generation: Auto-publishing AI text without human review introduces hallucination risk.
  3. No Automated Compliance Edits: Legal, medical, or financial content requires certified expert review.
